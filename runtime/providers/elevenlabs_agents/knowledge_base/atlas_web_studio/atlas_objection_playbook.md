# Atlas Objection Playbook

Owns common Atlas objections and response direction. Keep responses short, mechanism-first, and non-guaranteed.

## Instagram already works

Do not attack Instagram. First use booking filter for people who do not follow yet. On repeat challenge, rotate to DM reduction. If Instagram fills the calendar and the buyer wants no more bookings, stop pushing.

## Google Maps already works

Agree that Maps can help discovery. Use trust-before-call, service-area page, tap-to-call, or comparison page as the practical difference. Do not promise rankings or more calls.

## referrals already work

Referrals can stay primary. Use trust-before-call or pre-qualification: referred people often still check the business before calling.

## current website already works

Ask one light fit question or disqualify. If the current site already gets the right calls, bookings, quote requests, or consultations and the buyer is happy, there may be no problem to solve.

## too expensive

Separate the free mockup from paid work. If asked about paid scope, use approved price anchors and cost drivers. Do not invent final pricing.

## no time

Compress. Offer to send the mockup or confirm a callback window. Do not run a full pitch while the buyer is busy.

## is this a scam

Be direct: the mockup is free to review, no obligation, and paid discussion happens only if they want to continue. Do not invent proof, client names, or urgency.

## guarantee demand

Say Atlas cannot guarantee outcomes. If the buyer only continues with guaranteed results, disqualify.

## pay-per-lead demand

Atlas is not selling a pay-per-lead guarantee in this package. If that is the only acceptable model, disqualify cleanly.

## SEO guarantee demand

Basic local search foundations can be part of the build. Guaranteed rankings, traffic, page-one placement, customers, or calls are not offered.

## previous bad agency experience

Do not reassure vaguely. Use risk reversal: free mockup first, no obligation, written paid scope before paid work, clear deliverables, timeline, revisions, ownership, and payment stages if a paid project is later discussed.
