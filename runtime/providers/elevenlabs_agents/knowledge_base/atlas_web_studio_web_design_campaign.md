# Atlas Web Studio Web Design Campaign Knowledge Base

Package: `ELEVENLABS-010-web-design-sales-control-repair`

This campaign knowledge base gives Atlas Web Studio website-specific facts and
selling points for local restaurant outreach. It is campaign material, not
universal sales method. Universal sales guidance explains how to sell; this file
explains what the Atlas Web Studio offer is worth in this campaign.

## Campaign Offer

Atlas Web Studio is offering a free homepage mockup idea for the business.

The mockup is a first look, not a finished website, contract, redesign, booking
system, or guarantee. The buyer should use it to judge whether the homepage
direction is clearer before deciding whether a paid website conversation is
worth it.

The core ask is:

- take a quick look at the free homepage mockup
- decide whether the direction is useful
- if useful, continue to a scoped website conversation

## Local Restaurant Website Value

A website should not be sold as magic. Sell it as a controlled homepage layout
for practical customer decisions.

For a restaurant, the useful value points are:

- Customers can avoid hunting through profile sections, posts, photos, or
  third-party listings when they need the practical next step.
- First-time visitors who are not already following the restaurant can still get
  a clear answer from a page the owner controls.
- The owner controls the page direction instead of depending only on profile
  layouts, social feeds, or scattered third-party listings.
- Google Maps and Instagram can stay useful; the website can give the owner a
  controlled page and shareable link instead of another feed or profile.
- The reservation phone number can be easier to notice before a customer gives
  up or calls with basic questions.
- Staff may get fewer basic information calls if the page answers the obvious
  questions before someone picks up the phone.
- The restaurant gets one simple link it can put in an Instagram bio, Google
  profile, email, text reply, QR code, menu, flyer, or printed material.
- Operational details can be shown in a page layout chosen for the restaurant
  instead of a generic profile layout.
- Menu presentation can be easier to scan than a photo, old post, story
  highlight, or third-party listing.
- The page can separate today's core details from optional future sections like
  catering, private events, gift cards, hiring, delivery links, or booking.
- A simple page can clarify what the business wants a visitor to do next: call,
  check hours, view the menu, find the location, or ask about a table.
- The mockup gives the owner something concrete to react to before spending
  money on a full site.

Use one value point at a time. Choose the point that matches the buyer's last
concern.

## When The Buyer Asks Why They Need A Website

Do not answer with a generic feature list. Pick the most relevant practical
outcome.

If the buyer says customers already find them on Google or Instagram:

`Those can stay useful. The website test is whether you get one page you
control and can share anywhere, not another feed or profile.`

If the buyer says Instagram already shows the menu:

`If Instagram already works for regulars, keep it. The website angle is for
people who want the menu without digging through posts, photos, or highlights.`

If the buyer asks what the website adds:

`It gives you a page layout you control, with one link you can use in your bio,
Google profile, texts, QR codes, or staff replies.`

If the buyer says people call if they need the restaurant:

`That makes sense. The point is to make the number and the basic details easier
to find before they call.`

If the buyer asks what a clearer customer decision path means:

`It means a first-time visitor can tell what kind of place it is, whether it
fits what they want, and what to do next without bouncing between profiles.`

If the buyer says the organization or layout point sounds vague:

`A practical reason is fewer basic calls to staff. If a page answers routine
questions before someone calls, the call can be about the table instead of the
basics.`

If the buyer asks what a website adds after Google Maps:

`Google gives people a profile. The website gives you a page you control and a
single link you can use anywhere.`

If the buyer says the website sounds like the same information again:

`The facts may be the same. The test is whether the page is easier to scan,
share, and control than the current profile path. If it is not, ignore the
mockup.`

If the buyer asks how this helps with customers:

`It will not guarantee more customers. The safer point is that a first-time
visitor can understand the basics faster and know what to do next.`

If the buyer asks why this is worth their time:

`Because the mockup lets you judge the direction before paying. If it looks like
the same thing you already have, you can ignore it.`

If the buyer cares about the menu specifically:

`Then the menu should be the focus. A clean homepage can make the menu easier to
scan than a photo or old post, with the reservation number close by.`

If the buyer asks what is actually on the mockup after comparing it to Instagram
or Google Maps:

`It should show the homepage direction around the thing you care about most. If
the menu is the concern, the menu should be the easiest part to judge. It should
not just copy your posts or Maps profile.`

If the buyer asks what the mockup shows that Instagram or Google Maps does not
already have:

`It may not be new facts. The test is whether a controlled homepage feels easier
to compare than the current profile path. If it feels like a duplicate, ignore
it.`

If the buyer asks why someone would click a website instead of the Instagram
link:

`Some customers may use Instagram, and that is fine. The website only matters if
it gives a faster first impression or cleaner shareable page. The mockup tests
that before you pay for anything.`

If the buyer asks about a useful future add-on:

`That could be future scope if you wanted it. The first mockup should stay
simple so you can judge the page direction before expanding the project.`

If the buyer keeps challenging why a website matters, rotate these distinct
angles without asking to review the mockup after every answer:

- control: a page and layout the restaurant owns
- staff time: fewer basic questions before reservation calls
- menu clarity: a menu that is easier to scan than posts, photos, or profile
  sections
- shareable link: one link for Instagram, Google, texts, emails, QR codes, and
  print
- first-time visitor clarity: someone who does not already know the restaurant
  can understand the basics before calling
- optional future scope: booking, catering, private events, gift cards, or
  ordering only if the buyer asks or describes that pain
- proof before purchase: the free mockup lets the owner judge before spending

If the buyer asks why they should spend time on a mockup:

`So you can judge the direction before paying for anything. If it does not make
the basics clearer, there is nothing to move forward with.`

## Google Maps And Instagram Boundary

Do not attack Google Maps, Instagram, Facebook, reviews, delivery apps, or any
existing channel. They may already be useful.

The approved framing is:

- not a replacement
- not an insult to current channels
- one controlled homepage layout
- a quick comparison against the current scattered path
- clearer next step before someone calls

Bad framing:

- `Instagram is not enough.`
- `Google Maps is the problem.`
- `A website will bring more customers.`
- `Your current online presence is weak.`

Better framing:

- `Those can stay. This is just a cleaner page direction to compare.`
- `I would not replace that. I would make the practical details easier to land on.`
- `The question is not whether Google is bad. It is whether a page you control
  is easier to scan, share, and judge than the current profile path.`

## Online Booking And Upsell Boundary

The current Mike's Kitchen campaign is phone-reservation first.

If the buyer explicitly says they do not want online booking, apps, ordering
systems, or online reservations, respect that. Do not upsell booking in that
conversation. Say phone reservations stay and the mockup focuses on making the
reservation phone number easier to find.

If the buyer is neutral or curious about online booking, it can be mentioned as
an optional future add-on, not as part of the current free mockup:

`That could be added later if you wanted it, especially if phone calls take too
much staff time. For this mockup, I would keep your phone reservation process
and just make it clearer.`

If the buyer complains that staff spend too much time answering basic calls,
the optional booking or form angle can be framed as future scope:

`If that becomes a real pain, a booking flow could reduce some back-and-forth.
But I would not push that into this first mockup unless you wanted it.`

Do not claim guaranteed lower workload. Use potential language only.

## Pricing Boundary

The free mockup has no obligation.

Approved pricing anchors for this demo:

- Simple website projects generally start around `$1,000`.
- Final price depends on pages, content, integrations, design depth, and scope.
- Premium or immersive work can go up to around `$5,000` depending on scope.
- Basic hosting for a small business website is usually around `$10-$30/month`.
- Domain costs are separate and should be confirmed before anything paid.

Only discuss pricing when the buyer asks about price, cost, money, catch,
hidden fees, surprise pricing, hosting, domain, ongoing fees, or what happens
after the free mockup.

If the buyer asks directly about hosting or monthly costs, answer the hosting
ballpark immediately. Do not make them ask multiple times.

## Claims To Avoid

Do not promise:

- more customers
- more calls
- more bookings
- more reservations
- higher revenue
- SEO ranking
- guaranteed traffic
- guaranteed workload reduction
- guaranteed social-media improvement

Use safer language:

- clearer
- easier to find
- less confusing
- a concrete direction to judge
- a cleaner next step
- a page the owner controls
