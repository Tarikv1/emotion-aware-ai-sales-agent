# Atlas Detailed Website Pricing Design

Date: 2026-07-11

## Objective

Give the Atlas Web Studio agent enough pricing detail to answer specific buyer questions accurately without volunteering prices, dumping a menu, mechanically stacking line items, or treating custom software as a fixed-price website feature.

The commercial model is a hybrid ladder:

- affordable template-based entry work;
- higher bands for original design and deeper content;
- guarded add-on ranges for compatible existing sites;
- whole-project bands for new builds;
- mandatory scoping for custom APIs, accounts, databases, portals, dashboards, and complex business logic.

Atlas includes light copy refinement from client-provided information. Full research-led copywriting is separate. Hosting and post-launch support are sold through separate care plans.

## Market Basis

The price policy targets US local businesses in USD. Public benchmarks show a wide market:

- Upwork lists personal or simple sites from a few hundred dollars and broader website projects commonly up to several thousand dollars: [Upwork 2026 website cost guide](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-much-does-it-cost-to-build-website).
- Upwork lists web-design rates around $15-$30 per hour at the marketplace median, with higher rates for CMS and full-custom work: [Upwork web designer rates](https://www.upwork.com/hire/web-designers/cost/).
- Forbes Advisor places a freelance basic site around $500-$3,000 and agency or custom-coded work around $3,000-$15,000+: [Forbes Advisor website design guide](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/business/software/how-design-website/).
- Bark's US guide places freelancer basic sites around $500-$2,000, small-business sites around $1,500-$5,000, and agency small-business sites around $3,000-$10,000: [Bark US web design guide](https://www.bark.com/en/us/web-design/).
- Clutch's verified agency data shows materially higher agency hourly rates and emphasizes size, features, style, copywriting, and SEO as cost drivers: [Clutch web design pricing](https://clutch.co/web-designers/pricing).

These sources are directional and commercially interested. The Atlas ranges are a deliberate product policy, not a claim that every provider charges the same amount.

## Price Disclosure Gate

Price disclosure is opt-in and buyer-triggered. Emma must not volunteer paid pricing during the opening, discovery, capability explanation, objection handling, mockup explanation, or closing path.

### Price-Triggering Buyer Intent

Emma may quote a price only when the buyer:

- asks how much something costs;
- asks what Atlas charges;
- requests a price, fee, range, ballpark, estimate, or starting point;
- asks whether a particular feature costs extra;
- asks about monthly charges, affordability, or budget fit;
- provides a concrete budget and asks whether the work fits;
- challenges or asks for clarification about a price Emma already gave.

### Non-Triggering Buyer Intent

The following do not permit paid-price disclosure:

- mentioning booking, payments, CRM, ecommerce, SEO, a portal, or another feature;
- asking whether Atlas can build or connect something;
- asking what the free mockup includes;
- asking what happens after the mockup;
- asking whether the mockup is free, has a catch, creates a contract, or causes follow-up calls;
- showing ordinary interest, agreement, curiosity, or uncertainty without a price question.

The word `free` in a mockup or no-obligation question is a process-risk trigger, not a paid-price trigger.

### Response Shape After A Price Trigger

Emma must:

1. give the closest relevant package or add-on range;
2. identify one meaningful scope driver;
3. ask at most one necessary clarifying question;
4. avoid reciting other packages;
5. avoid calculating a final quote on the call.

## Quoting Algorithm

### Step 1: Determine Context

- New website: choose a whole-project package.
- Compatible existing website: an add-on range may be appropriate.
- Context unclear: ask whether this is a new website or an addition to an existing site.

### Step 2: Classify Implementation

- Simple: native form, embed, ordinary content, or supported plugin.
- Integrated: data moves between systems or triggers an automation.
- Custom: API work, accounts, databases, permissions, nonstandard security, or original business logic.

### Step 3: Select One Range

- Quote one package or one add-on range.
- Name one driver that could move the price within that range.
- Ask at most one clarifying question when it materially changes the classification.

### Step 4: Prevent Mechanical Stacking

- One or two independent add-ons may receive separate ranges.
- Three or more meaningful additions move the conversation to the closest whole-project package.
- Overlapping work must not be charged twice.
- Emma must not add ranges together into a final project total during the initial call.

### Step 5: Escalate Custom Scope

Portals, dashboards, custom APIs, user-account systems, databases, complex payments, inventory synchronization, multivendor systems, and original calculators with material business logic require scoping. Emma may explain likely cost drivers but must not promise a fixed price or ceiling.

## Range Selection

The lower end is appropriate when:

- the buyer supplies organized copy and assets;
- the project uses a supported platform and existing design system;
- page count and revisions are limited;
- the integration is standard and documented.

The upper end is appropriate when:

- more original design work is required;
- content needs significant rewriting or migration;
- more pages, variants, or revision rounds are required;
- integrations require field mapping, automation, testing, or error handling.

## Excluded Costs

The quoted website and add-on ranges exclude unless expressly included:

- domain registration;
- third-party platform subscriptions;
- premium plugins and application fees;
- transaction and payment-processing fees;
- paid stock assets;
- custom photography or video;
- full branding;
- translation;
- advertising spend;
- ongoing SEO campaigns;
- taxes.

Emma should mention exclusions only when relevant to the buyer's price question, not as an automatic disclaimer dump.

## Example Decisions

### Capability Without Price

Buyer: "Can you add booking?"

Emma: "Yes. That can be a simple appointment request or a live calendar integration, depending on what you need."

No price is permitted.

### Existing-Site Add-On Price

Buyer: "How much would the simple booking version cost on our current site?"

Emma: "A simple appointment-request setup on a compatible existing site is usually around $100-$250. A live calendar connection is a different scope."

### New-Site Feature Price

Buyer: "What would a new site with booking cost?"

Emma: "A straightforward new local-business site with a simple appointment-request form is usually around $900-$1,500. If it needs a live calendar integration or a more custom design, it can move toward $1,800-$3,000 or $2,800-$4,500 depending on the system."

### CRM Integration

Buyer: "What does a direct CRM connection cost?"

Emma: "On a compatible existing site, a direct CRM integration is usually $1,000-$2,500 or more, depending on the API and data flow. A new website with the integration generally falls in the $4,000-$6,500 band."

### Mockup Process Question

Buyer: "Is the mockup really free?"

Emma: "Yes. There is no payment or contract for receiving the mockup."

No paid price is permitted.

### Custom Portal

Buyer: "How much for a parent portal?"

Emma: "That is custom application work. I would need to scope the accounts, data, permissions, security, and integrations before giving you a responsible range."

## Runtime Architecture

- the hard price-disclosure gate;
- simple, integrated, and custom classification;
- whole-project versus existing-site add-on selection;
- the one-range response rule;
- the no-stacking rule;
- the mandatory-scope categories.

The complete package and feature matrix belongs in one focused active pricing KB document. Existing pricing documents should not repeat conflicting dollar values.

Top-level campaign variables should mirror the approved package bands needed by existing prompt templates. A validator must ensure the variables, pricing KB, campaign facts, and live dynamic-variable defaults do not drift.

The active KB attachment set and order must remain unchanged. Existing documents should be updated in place rather than creating duplicate provider documents.
