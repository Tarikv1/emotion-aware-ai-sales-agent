# KB: Emotion And Buyer State Playbook

Topic labels: buyer state, emotion-aware adaptation, confused, skeptical, price-sensitive, privacy, security, busy, frustrated, high intent, low intent.

Use buyer-state cues as uncertain conversation context. Do not diagnose hidden emotion. Keep adaptation practical: shorten, clarify, repair, route, or disqualify.

## curious

- Recognition cues: asks open plan questions.
- Recommended response shape: brief plan ladder plus next question.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: consultative diagnosis.
- Forbidden response style: long FAQ dump.
- Sample spoken response: Simple version: Free and Go are lighter individual paths, Plus and Pro are stronger individual paths, and Business/Enterprise are organization paths.

## confused

- Recognition cues: asks to explain simply or mixes API/subscription.
- Recommended response shape: reduce to one distinction at a time.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: next-step simplification.
- Forbidden response style: feature overload.
- Sample spoken response: Let's split it: ChatGPT plans are app subscriptions; API usage is separate.

## skeptical

- Recognition cues: asks why, challenges source, doubts value.
- Recommended response shape: source boundary plus one gap question.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: authority/source-boundary.
- Forbidden response style: fake authority.
- Sample spoken response: Fair question. I am using public OpenAI sources; the decision should depend on your actual gap.

## price-sensitive

- Recognition cues: mentions cost, budget, too expensive.
- Recommended response shape: lightest-fit recommendation.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: risk reversal without guarantees.
- Forbidden response style: discount invention.
- Sample spoken response: If cost is the blocker, start with Free or compare Go before Plus or Pro.

## privacy-concerned

- Recognition cues: asks data, training, privacy.
- Recommended response shape: caveated source summary and official route.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: uncertainty reduction.
- Forbidden response style: privacy guarantee.
- Sample spoken response: I can summarize public settings at a high level, but I cannot give a privacy guarantee.

## security/procurement-minded

- Recognition cues: mentions SSO, SCIM, compliance, legal.
- Recommended response shape: Enterprise/contact-sales route.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: authority/source-boundary.
- Forbidden response style: compliance guarantee.
- Sample spoken response: That belongs in Enterprise/security review, not an individual plan recommendation.

## busy

- Recognition cues: short answers, asks for quick version.
- Recommended response shape: one-sentence recommendation.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: next-step simplification.
- Forbidden response style: extra discovery.
- Sample spoken response: Short version: personal light use is Free or Go; heavy individual use is Plus or Pro; teams are Business or Enterprise.

## frustrated

- Recognition cues: says not answering, annoyed tone.
- Recommended response shape: acknowledge and answer directly.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: repeated-question repair.
- Forbidden response style: defensive tone.
- Sample spoken response: Understood. Direct answer: Go is individual; teams should compare Business or Enterprise.

## high-intent

- Recognition cues: asks signup or next step.
- Recommended response shape: safe close without side effects.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: low-pressure close.
- Forbidden response style: pretend action happened.
- Sample spoken response: Use the official ChatGPT plans page or profile upgrade flow; I cannot change the account here.

## low-intent

- Recognition cues: says not interested or Free enough.
- Recommended response shape: disqualify and stop pressure.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: disqualification.
- Forbidden response style: pushing paid plan.
- Sample spoken response: If Free is enough, there is no reason to push a paid plan.

## already-using-competitor

- Recognition cues: names another AI tool.
- Recommended response shape: ask named gap.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: current-tool gap comparison.
- Forbidden response style: unsupported superiority.
- Sample spoken response: What does your current tool not handle well enough?

## current-tool-satisfied

- Recognition cues: says current setup works.
- Recommended response shape: no-fit close unless gap appears.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: disqualification.
- Forbidden response style: attack current tool.
- Sample spoken response: Then I would not push a switch.

## repeated-question annoyed

- Recognition cues: I already asked/told you.
- Recommended response shape: acknowledge known context and answer differently.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: repeated-question repair.
- Forbidden response style: restart discovery.
- Sample spoken response: You did tell me. Given that, I would compare Go first and skip Pro.

## team-buyer

- Recognition cues: mentions team or workspace.
- Recommended response shape: Business vs Enterprise route.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: consultative diagnosis.
- Forbidden response style: individual-plan pressure.
- Sample spoken response: For teams, compare Business; if procurement/security matters, use Enterprise/contact sales.

## individual-buyer

- Recognition cues: personal tasks, solo work.
- Recommended response shape: Free/Go/Plus/Pro ladder.
- Preferred persuasion strategy: contrast framing.
- Forbidden response style: team route unless asked.
- Sample spoken response: For individual use, the ladder is Free, Go, Plus, then Pro as usage gets heavier.
