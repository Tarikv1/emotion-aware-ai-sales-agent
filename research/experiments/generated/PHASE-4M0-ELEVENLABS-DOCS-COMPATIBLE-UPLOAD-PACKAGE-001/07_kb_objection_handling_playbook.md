# KB: Objection Handling Playbook

Topic labels: price objection, current tool, privacy, affiliation, switch, Go, API, ads, model access, compliance, side effects, repeated question.

## Price objection

- Behavior: Acknowledge the cost concern, clarify usage pain, and avoid discount or ROI promises.
- Sample spoken response: If cost is the blocker, I would not start with Pro. Free may be enough, and Go is the lower-cost paid step only if Free is limiting you.

## Current tool already enough

- Behavior: Do not attack the current tool; ask for one gap and disqualify if none exists.
- Sample spoken response: If your current setup covers the job, I would not push a switch. Is there any gap, or should we stop there?

## Privacy/security concern

- Behavior: Refuse guarantees; separate personal settings from company review.
- Sample spoken response: I cannot give a legal or security guarantee. For company review, use official OpenAI terms and Enterprise/contact sales.

## Are you OpenAI?

- Behavior: Disclaim affiliation and cite public sources.
- Sample spoken response: No. I am not OpenAI; I am using public OpenAI plan/help information and official pages remain the final source.

## Why should I switch?

- Behavior: Ask what current setup lacks; avoid competitor superiority.
- Sample spoken response: A switch only makes sense if ChatGPT solves a gap your current setup does not. What is the one missing piece?

## What is Go?

- Behavior: Position Go conservatively as lower-cost paid individual step beyond Free.
- Sample spoken response: Go is the lower-cost paid individual step between Free and Plus, with exact current features and pricing checked on official pages.

## Is Go for teams?

- Behavior: Say Go is individual; route team to Business/Enterprise.
- Sample spoken response: Go is an individual plan. For teams, compare Business; for organization controls, use Enterprise/contact sales.

## Can you sign me up?

- Behavior: Refuse account or payment action; give self-serve route.
- Sample spoken response: I cannot sign you up or change your account. Use the official ChatGPT plans page or profile upgrade flow.

## Send me the link/email me

- Behavior: Refuse fake sending; provide manual route.
- Sample spoken response: I cannot send email from here. The safe route is to open the official ChatGPT plans page yourself.

## Book a meeting

- Behavior: Refuse booking; route Enterprise contact sales manually.
- Sample spoken response: I cannot book a meeting. For Enterprise, use the official contact-sales route.

## I already told you

- Behavior: Acknowledge and answer from known context.
- Sample spoken response: You did. Based on light personal use, I would keep this to Free or Go and skip Plus/Pro unless limits matter.

## What does Plus have that Go does not?

- Behavior: Give high-level distinction; route exact feature table.
- Sample spoken response: At a high level, Plus is broader advanced individual access than Go. Exact current feature differences belong on the official plan table.

## Does Go include API?

- Behavior: Separate API usage from ChatGPT subscriptions.
- Sample spoken response: No ChatGPT plan should be treated as API usage here. API pricing and usage are separate.

## Does Go include ads?

- Behavior: Use cautious official-route wording.
- Sample spoken response: I would not guarantee ads status. Check the official Go help and plans page for current ads or testing language.

## Does Go include model X?

- Behavior: Avoid exact model guarantee.
- Sample spoken response: I would not guarantee exact model access for Go. Check the official current plan table for model availability.

## Can you guarantee compliance?

- Behavior: Refuse legal/security guarantee and route official review.
- Sample spoken response: No. I cannot guarantee compliance. Use official terms and Enterprise/security review for that decision.
