# Objection Handling Playbook

Use a short pattern: acknowledge, reframe to the low-risk mockup, ask one useful question, close for permission. Do not argue.

## "We already have a website."

Response: "That may be enough. The question is whether it is helping phone visitors call, book, or request a quote. We can send a free homepage mockup showing a sharper version, and you can ignore it if it is not useful."

Close: "Would it be useful if we sent over a free homepage mockup so you can compare?"

## "We do not need a website."

Response: "That can be true if referrals and walk-ins are already enough. If people still look you up before deciding, a simple page can make you easier to trust and contact."

Close: "Should I send a small mockup just so you can see the difference?"

## "We use Instagram/Facebook."

Response: "Instagram/Facebook is useful for attention. A website is better for the stable details people check before acting: services, menu, booking, location, reviews, and contact."

Close: "Would a free mockup that connects your social traffic to a clearer booking or call path be useful?"

## "Too expensive."

Response: "That is why the first step is not a paid project. The free mockup helps you decide whether the direction is worth discussing before any scope or price."

Close: "Would you be open to reviewing the free mockup first?"

## "Send me info."

Response: "I can. The more useful version is a quick custom homepage mockup, because it shows what this could look like for your business instead of a generic brochure."

Close: "What email should we send it to?"

## "I'm busy."

Response: "Understood. I will keep it short. We can send a free mockup, and you can review it when convenient."

Close: "Who should we send it to?"

## "Who are you?"

Response: "I am calling from [AGENCY_NAME]. We build websites for local businesses and are offering a free homepage mockup as a low-risk first step."

Close: "Would it be useful to see a mockup before deciding whether a full site is worth discussing?"

## "Is this spam?"

Response: "No. This is an internal test script for [AGENCY_NAME]'s sales process, and in a real call we would identify ourselves clearly, respect stop requests, and avoid fake claims or pressure."

Close: "If you are not interested, I can stop here."

## "Can you guarantee leads?"

Response: "No. We should not promise that. A website can improve the path from visitor to call, booking, quote request, or walk-in, but results depend on the business, market, offer, traffic, and follow-up."

Close: "The safe first step is a free mockup so you can judge whether the path looks stronger."

## "Will you rank us #1 on Google?"

Response: "No one should promise that. We can build local SEO basics into the site: clear service pages, location info, headings, contact paths, and trust signals."

Close: "Would a mockup showing those basics be worth reviewing?"

## "We had a bad experience with an agency."

Response: "That is a fair concern. This is intentionally low-risk: review a free mockup first, then decide whether the style and thinking are credible."

Close: "Would you be open to judging the mockup before making any commitment?"

## "I need to ask my partner."

Response: "That makes sense. The mockup can make that conversation easier because it gives both of you something concrete to react to."

Close: "Who should review the mockup with you?"

## "Call me later."

Response: "I can note that as a follow-up preference in this test script, but no calendar or calling tool is enabled here."

Close: "Would sending a free mockup be better than another call?"

## "We already have someone."

Response: "If they are doing strong work, staying with them may be the right move. A free mockup can still give you a comparison point without changing anything."

Close: "Would a comparison mockup be useful, or should we leave it there?"
