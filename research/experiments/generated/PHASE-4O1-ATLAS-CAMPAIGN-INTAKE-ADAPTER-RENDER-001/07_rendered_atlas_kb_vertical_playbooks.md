# Atlas Vertical Playbooks KB

## Restaurants With No Website

Wedge: Google and Instagram help people find the restaurant; the website helps them act.

Use:

- menu
- hours
- location
- photos
- reviews
- booking, order, or call path

Close: "Would it be useful to see a free homepage mockup with menu, hours, photos, reviews, and a clear booking, order, or call path?"

## Beauty Salons With Instagram Or Booking App

Wedge: Instagram gets attention; a website turns that interest into booking.

Use:

- services
- price or service types
- reviews
- photos
- one booking path

If buyer has a booking app, do not attack it. Position the website as the trust and decision layer before someone books.

Close: "Would a mockup showing services, photos, reviews, and one booking path be useful to compare?"

## Plumbers And Emergency Services

Wedge: urgent buyers choose whoever looks trustworthy and easiest to call from mobile.

Use:

- mobile click-to-call
- emergency service section
- service area
- reviews and trust
- quote or call path

Do not imply CRM, secure lead handling, email, or lead-processing tools.

Close: "Would it be useful to see a mockup built around emergency calls, service area, trust, and quote requests?"

## Strong Current Website

If the buyer says the current site works and names no problem, disqualify politely.

Say: "If the current site is already doing the job, that may be a no-fit, and I do not want to push."

If the buyer re-engages with curiosity, answer briefly and ask permission before offering a mockup again.

Close only after permission: "Would you be open to seeing one homepage direction, or should we leave it there?"

## Other Local Services

Use the same pattern:

- identify the visitor's decision moment
- name the trust and action elements
- offer a free homepage mockup/demo
- avoid unsupported guarantees
