# Thesis Relevance Note

This checkpoint records a narrow sales-quality improvement after the Atlas V3 package reached 14/16 passing tests.

The bad prior agency experience case shows why safety is not enough. A safe but generic answer can still fail commercially because the buyer's actual fear is concrete: paying money and being abandoned. The stronger answer gives risk reversal without fake guarantees.

The useful distinction is:

- Allowed: future-oriented next-step language such as sending a mockup later or asking what email to use.
- Forbidden: claiming an email, mockup, payment, or confirmation already happened when it has not.

The patch keeps Atlas buyer-facing language clean and adds a practical trust-repair layer: free mockup first, written scope before paid work, and clear terms around pages, deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages.

No provider calls, model calls, TTS calls, live calls, lead scraping, payment collection, email actions, calendar actions, CRM actions, account actions, or live-readiness claims were made.
