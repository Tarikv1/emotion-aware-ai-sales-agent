# Atlas Trust Repair And Risk Reversal KB V3

Use this when a buyer has been burned by an agency, worries about being ghosted, asks about deposits, or needs a partner to review. The goal is not reassurance. The goal is concrete risk reduction.

## Core Pattern

1. Acknowledge without defensiveness.
2. Agree that blind trust is unreasonable.
3. Reduce risk with the free mockup first.
4. Explain that paid work should come after a written scope.
5. Name what the scope should cover: pages, deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages.
6. Do not promise legal terms or guarantees.
7. Do not collect payment.
8. Move to one concrete next step.

Required example:

"That's a fair concern. I would not ask you to trust us blindly. The first step is free, so there is no payment risk. If you like the mockup, the next step would be a written scope before any paid build: pages, deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages. You should know exactly what you're paying for before putting money down."

## I had a bad experience with another agency.

Use this when the buyer says a previous agency wasted time, overpromised, or did not deliver.

Response:

"If the last agency disappeared, the safest path is not to pay anything today. Review the free mockup first. If the direction looks useful, then we discuss a written project scope before any paid build. That scope should make clear what gets built, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages."

Close path: ask whether they want the mockup sent to them first or to another reviewer.

## They took my money and disappeared.

Response:

"I understand why that would make you cautious. I would not ask you to put money down from a phone call. The free mockup comes first. If it looks useful, then the paid conversation should be based on a written scope with pages, deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages."

Close path: ask what business name and email should be used for the free mockup.

## How do I know you won't ghost me?

Response:

"That is exactly why the first step should be concrete and low-risk. The mockup lets you judge whether the direction is useful before you commit. If it moves forward, the next step should be a clear written scope, not a vague promise."

Close path: ask which email they want used for the mockup.

## Do I need to pay a deposit?

Response:

"We do not need to discuss payment on this call. The free mockup comes first. If you like it, the paid step would only make sense after a written scope and payment structure are clear."

Close path: ask for the business name or reviewer for the free mockup.

## What do I get before paying?

Response:

"Before paying anything, you should get the free mockup so you can judge the direction. If you like it, the next paid step should be based on a written scope that names pages, deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages."

Close path: ask what part of the current site or homepage worries them most.

## Will there be a written scope or contract?

Response:

"Before any paid build, there should be a written scope. It should make clear the pages, deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages. I would not ask you to rely on a vague promise."

Close path: ask whether the mockup should be reviewed by the buyer or a partner first.

## Who owns the site?

Response:

"Ownership should be clear before paid work starts. That belongs in the written scope, along with deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, and payment stages. The free mockup comes first, so you can judge the direction before that paid conversation."

Close path: ask who should review the mockup.

## What happens after the free mockup?

Response:

"After the mockup, you decide whether the direction is useful. If it is not useful, there is no paid step to discuss. If it is useful, the next step should be a written scope before any paid build: pages, deliverables, timeline, revision rounds, ownership, and payment stages."

Close path: ask whether the buyer wants the mockup sent to them or another reviewer.

## I don't trust agencies.

Response:

"That is reasonable if you have seen agencies overpromise. I would not ask for blind trust. The safer path is to review the free mockup first, then only discuss paid work if the direction is concrete enough to put into a written scope."

Close path: ask what would make the mockup useful enough to judge.

## I need to ask my partner.

Response:

"The mockup can give your partner something concrete to judge instead of just hearing a sales pitch. You can review it first, or we can send it to whichever email you prefer."

Close path: ask whether the buyer or partner should review the mockup first.

## Do Not Say

Do not say Trust us.

Do not say We guarantee satisfaction.

Do not say We never disappear.

Do not say You are fully protected.

Do not say No contract is needed.

Do not say The email has been sent.

Do not say The mockup is already created.

Do not say Payment is processed.
