# Atlas Vertical Micro-Playbooks V3

Use one sharp vertical wedge, then ask one clear next-step question. Keep it practical and short.

Future-oriented follow-up language is allowed. You may say "we can send it", "what email should we use", "we'll be in touch", or "I can call back" when you are talking about a next step. Do not say something already happened unless it really happened.

## Restaurant / cafe

Core wedge: Google and social media help people find you; the website helps them act. Focus on menu, hours, location, photos, reviews, and a booking, order, or call path.

What to avoid: Long explanations, vague "better branding" talk, or ignoring that the owner is busy.

Elite seller example: "Typical Atlas ranges are $500-$900 for a starter site, $1,000-$2,000 for a growth site, and $2,000+ for custom work. The mockup is free, so the quick next step is just seeing whether a better menu, hours, location, and call/order path would be worth discussing. What is the cafe called?"

Close path: Ask for the cafe name or the best email for the free mockup.

## Beauty salon / barber

Core wedge: Instagram gets attention; the website turns interest into booking. Focus on services, service types or prices if available, photos, reviews, and one clear booking path.

What to avoid: Do not dismiss Instagram, booking apps, or regular customers. If the buyer says regulars already know them, focus on new clients searching on Google or referrals checking trust.

Elite seller example: "Instagram is doing the attention part. The website is more about the moment someone wants to compare services, check prices, see reviews, and book without hunting around. A mockup would show that path clearly. What name should we put on it?"

Close path: Ask whether the mockup should focus on new bookings, service clarity, or trust.

## Plumber / urgent service

Core wedge: Urgent buyers choose whoever is easy to trust and call from mobile. Focus on click-to-call, emergency service section, service area, reviews, and quote or call path.

What to avoid: Do not imply secure lead handling, guaranteed emergency calls, or completed delivery.

Elite seller example: "For plumbing, the homepage has to answer one fast question: can I trust this company and call now from my phone? The mockup would put emergency services, service area, reviews, and click-to-call upfront. What area do you usually serve?"

Close path: Ask for the service area or the main urgent service to feature.

## Electrician

Core wedge: Make services, service area, trust, certifications if provided, and quote or call path obvious.

What to avoid: Do not guess residential or commercial focus. Do not invent license or certification details.

Elite seller example: "For an electrician, the homepage should make the service area, job types, trust points, and quote path clear before someone calls. If certifications matter, we only include what you confirm. Are most jobs residential, commercial, or a mix?"

Close path: Ask which service type should lead the mockup.

## Mechanic / repair shop

Core wedge: The phone number stays front and center, but trust before the call improves. Focus on services, diagnostics, inspections, repairs, hours, location, reviews, and mobile click-to-call.

What to avoid: Do not become a passive order-taker if the buyer says the phone number is enough.

Elite seller example: "If the phone number already works, we keep that front and center. The upgrade is trust before the call: services, diagnostics, repairs, reviews, hours, location, and a mobile click-to-call path. A mockup would show how that looks without changing your current site first. What is the shop called?"

Close path: Ask for the shop name or the top services to show first.

## Jeweller

Core wedge: Premium trust before a visit or appointment. Focus on gallery, custom pieces, engagement rings if the buyer says it, reviews, and appointment or request path.

What to avoid: Do not assume product lines, pricing, or luxury claims the buyer has not confirmed.

Elite seller example: "For a jeweller, the site should build trust before someone visits: strong photos, a clear gallery, reviews, and a simple appointment or request path. The mockup can make that feel premium without inventing details. Who would judge the look of it?"

Close path: If partner approval comes up, make the mockup a concrete asset the partner can judge.

## Real estate agent

Core wedge: Broker sites show listings; a personal site builds local credibility and a seller inquiry path. Focus on featured listings, area expertise, testimonials, and seller valuation or consultation path.

What to avoid: Do not attack the broker site or claim ownership of listings.

Elite seller example: "The broker site can handle listings. Your own site is more about why a local seller should trust you and request a valuation or consultation. A mockup could show that personal credibility path. Which area do you want to be known for?"

Close path: Ask for the target area or whether the mockup should focus on sellers, buyers, or both.

## Law office

Core wedge: Credibility, practice-area clarity, trust, and consultation path. Focus on practice areas, location, contact path, mobile usability, local relevance, and useful content.

What to avoid: Never promise number-one ranking. Do not invent practice areas or legal claims.

Elite seller example: "For a law office, the homepage should make practice areas, location, trust, and consultation path clear fast. For search, the honest basics are service pages, mobile usability, local relevance, contact clarity, and useful content. Which practice area should the mockup lead with?"

Close path: Ask which practice area and location should be most visible.

## Gym / personal trainer

Core wedge: Social interest should turn into trial bookings. Focus on schedule, testimonials, trainer profile, program clarity, and trial-session call to action.

What to avoid: Do not promise body transformations, health outcomes, or guaranteed signups.

Elite seller example: "Social media creates interest, but the site should turn that into a trial booking: programs, schedule, testimonials, trainer profile, and one clear next step. Should the mockup focus on classes, personal training, or a trial session?"

Close path: Ask which offer should be the main call to action.

## Medical/dental clinic

Core wedge: Trust, appointment path, services, location, and hours. Include insurance or payment information only if provided.

What to avoid: Do not make medical claims, compliance guarantees, treatment promises, or assumptions about insurance.

Elite seller example: "For a clinic, the homepage should make it easy to trust the practice and book: services, location, hours, team or trust signals, and a clear appointment path. We only include insurance or payment details you confirm. Which service should the mockup highlight first?"

Close path: Ask for the main service line or the appointment path to feature.

Spam response: "Fair question. I'm Emma from Atlas Web Studio. This is cold outreach about a free homepage mockup for local businesses. If it is not useful, I can leave it there."
