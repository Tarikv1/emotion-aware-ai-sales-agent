# Objection Handling Playbook

Use this pattern: concise acknowledgement, business-value reframe, low-risk mockup close, clean exit if uninterested. Do not argue.

## "We already have a website."

Response: "That may be enough. The question is whether it is helping phone visitors call, book, or request a quote. A free mockup would give you a quick comparison without changing anything."

Close: "Would it be useful to compare your current site against a sharper homepage direction?"

Exit: "If the current site is already doing the job, we can leave it there."

## "We do not need a website."

Response: "That can be true if referrals and walk-ins are enough. If people still look you up before deciding, a simple site can make you easier to trust and contact."

Close: "Should we prepare a small mockup just so you can see the difference?"

Exit: "If online trust is not a priority, no problem."

## "We use Instagram/Facebook."

Response: "Instagram/Facebook is useful for attention. A website is better for stable details people check before acting: services, menu, booking, location, reviews, and contact."

Close: "Would a free mockup that connects your social traffic to a clearer booking or call path be useful?"

Exit: "If social is already covering that, we can leave it."

## "Too expensive."

Response: "That is exactly why the first step is not a paid project. The mockup helps you decide whether the direction is worth a pricing conversation."

Close: "Would you be open to reviewing the free mockup first?"

Exit: "If there is no interest even in seeing the direction, I will not push."

## "Send me info."

Response: "I can keep it simple. The more useful version is a quick custom homepage mockup, because it shows what this could look like for your business instead of a generic brochure."

Close: "Who would be the best person to review it?"

Exit: "If now is not the time, I can leave it there."

## "I'm busy."

Response: "Understood. I will keep it brief. We build sites that help local businesses turn phone visitors into calls, bookings, or quote requests."

Close: "Would a free mockup be worth reviewing when you have time?"

Exit: "If not, I will let you get back to work."

## "Who are you?"

Response: "I am calling from [AGENCY_NAME]. We build websites for local businesses, and the first step is simply a free homepage mockup."

Close: "Would seeing a mockup be useful before deciding whether a full site is worth discussing?"

Exit: "If you are not interested, I can stop here."

## "Is this spam?"

Response: "Fair question. I am calling from [AGENCY_NAME]. We build websites for local businesses, and I am only asking whether a free homepage mockup would be useful."

Close: "If not, I can leave it there."

Exit: "No problem. Thanks for your time."

## "Can you guarantee leads?"

Response: "No. No one should promise that. A better site can improve the path from visitor to call, booking, quote request, or walk-in, but results depend on the business, traffic, offer, and follow-up."

Close: "The safe first step is a free mockup so you can judge whether the path looks stronger."

Exit: "If a hard lead promise is required, we are probably not the right fit."

## "Will you rank us #1 on Google?"

Response: "No one credible should promise #1. What we can do is build the local basics clearly: service pages, location info, headings, contact paths, and trust signals."

Close: "Would a mockup showing those basics be worth reviewing?"

Exit: "If only a ranking promise matters, we should leave it there."

## "We had a bad agency experience."

Response: "That is a fair concern. This is intentionally low-risk: review a free mockup first, then decide whether the thinking and style are credible."

Close: "Would you be open to judging the mockup before making any commitment?"

Exit: "If you do not want to revisit websites right now, I understand."

## "I need to ask my partner."

Response: "That makes sense. The mockup can make that conversation easier because it gives both of you something concrete to react to."

Close: "Who should review the mockup with you?"

Exit: "If partner review is not worth it, I will leave it there."

## "Call me later."

Response: "No problem. A mockup may be easier than another call because you can review it when convenient."

Close: "Who would normally review something like that?"

Exit: "If you would rather not continue, I will stop here."

## "We already have someone."

Response: "If they are doing strong work, staying with them may be right. A free mockup can still give you a comparison point without replacing anyone."

Close: "Would a comparison mockup be useful, or should we leave it there?"

Exit: "If your current person has it handled, no problem."

## "Wrong person."

Response: "Thanks for saying that. I do not want to pitch the wrong person."

Close: "Who normally reviews the website or marketing decisions?"

Exit: "If there is no one appropriate to speak with, I will leave it there."

## "Stop calling."

Response: "Understood. I will stop. Thanks for your time."

Close: "No further close."

Exit: "End the conversation immediately."
